AME$(4, 6)$ State – Real-Valued Representatives on a $3\times 3\times 3$ Rubik's Cube

update: 2026-10-14

This is just for fun. A $3\times3\times3$ Rubik's cube serves here as a classical model of a family of locally equivalent, real-valued AME$(4,6)$ states. Four marked corner cubies carry the local frames of the four six-level parties: $A$ starts at the corner RUF (right, up, front), $B$ at LUB, $C$ at LDF and $D$ at RDB. The solved cube encodes a reference state, which is already absolutely maximally entangled. A twist applies a real orthogonal $6\times 6$ matrix to every party whose corner lies in the turned layer, so the state changes but remains AME. This model does not create entanglement.

details

AME$(4,6)$ means four parties $A$, $B$, $C$ and $D$ with six levels each, such that every two-party reduction is maximally mixed. The solved cube is assigned a real reference state $|\psi_0\rangle$ with $192$ nonzero coefficients, the tensor encoded by the pictures G02, G03 and G04 of the gallery. A configuration $\kappa$ of the cube gives each marked corner $p$ a frame $R_p(\kappa)$, the rotation that carries the corner from its home to its current position and orientation, and the decoded state is \[ |\psi(\kappa)\rangle=O\big(R_A(\kappa)\big) \otimes O\big(R_B(\kappa)\big) \otimes O\big(R_C(\kappa)\big) \otimes O\big(R_D(\kappa)\big)|\psi_0\rangle. \]

The six levels come from the four body diagonals of the cube. A rotation $R$ permutes them. If $P(R)$ is this $4\times 4$ permutation matrix, then $D(R)=\bigwedge^2 P(R)$ acts on the six oriented pairs $01$, $02$, $03$, $12$, $13$ and $23$ as a signed permutation matrix, with a minus sign whenever a pair is reordered. Moreover, $D(RS)=D(R)D(S)$. The local gate is $O(R)=W D(R) W^{\mathsf T}$, where $W={\rm H}\oplus\mathbb{I}_4$ with the Hadamard matrix ${\rm H}$ in the mixed real basis and $W=\mathbb{I}_6$ in the signed pair basis. Both bases start from the same $|\psi_0\rangle$. Every $O(R)$ is a real orthogonal matrix, hence a local unitary, although its determinant may be $-1$. A twist with spatial rotation $Q$ changes $R_p\mapsto Q R_p$ for the marked corners in the turned layer and leaves the other parties alone. Which parties move depends on $\kappa$, so the cube does not give a fixed representation of the Rubik group. The corner positions act as classical control.

Twists change the amplitudes, their signs, the number of nonzero entries and the overlap with $|\psi_0\rangle$. They cannot change the AME property, because \begin{equation} \rho_{XY}(\kappa)=\big(O_X\otimes O_Y\big)\frac{\mathbb{I}_{36}}{36}\big(O_X\otimes O_Y\big)^{\mathsf T}=\frac{\mathbb{I}_{36}}{36} \end{equation} for every pair of parties $X$ and $Y$. A quarter turn $Q$ permutes the four diagonals in a four-cycle, so $\operatorname{tr}D(Q)=\frac{1}{2}\big((\operatorname{tr}P(Q))^2-\operatorname{tr}P(Q)^2\big)=0$ and $\operatorname{tr}O(Q)=0$. From the solved cube a quarter turn moves exactly two marked corners. Since their joint reduction is $\mathbb{I}_{36}/36$, \begin{equation} \langle\psi_0|\psi(Q)\rangle=\frac{\operatorname{tr}O(Q) \operatorname{tr}O(Q)}{36}=0 . \end{equation} The same holds for every quarter turn that moves one or two marked corners, so the new state is again orthogonal to the previous one. Nothing of this kind is claimed for turns that move three or four marked corners.

The decoder reads only the four marked corners and ignores the edges and the other four corners. Hence, a solved cube always yields $|\psi_0\rangle$ but the converse is false.
